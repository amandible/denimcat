This document contains the rules for Mint Condition as of September 10, 2026. This
is a game I'm designing myself; the rules are in flux and will likely change.

The game seats 2-4 players.

Each player has a personal deck of money cards, numbered 1-13. (In my physical
prototype each player gets one suit of a poker deck, using A=1, J=11, Q=12,
K=13.)

The players will complete, over a series of auctions, to buy prize cards.

At the start of the game, the six price slots are set up. In the current version, this is
done by taking another 1-13 set of cards -- the six prices for this game are set this way:
- Draw three cards from the deck; these, taken individually, will be three of the prices.
- Draw two cards and add them together. This will be another price.
- Draw two more cards and add them together. This will be a fifth price.
- Draw three cards and add them together; if that is not yet the highest price on the table,
draw a fourth card and add it as well. That will be the sixth price.
Arrange these prices from highest to lowest.

Draw three prizes and put them in the second, third and fourth price slots. (Each slot
can hold one or more prizes, or be empty.) Each player draws two cards from their
money deck. The game begins.

Each auction starts with the player who won the previous one. (For the first auction of the
game, choose randomly.) To bid, a player must say a number that they could pay exactly
by spending one or more cards from their hand; they may also pass, in which case they
may not bid again in this auction. Following players in turn may raise the bid (saying a
higher number, that they could also pay exactly) or pass. When all but one player have
passed, the winning player pays their bid (discarding the cards) and takes their choice
of prizes from the display, but they may only take a prize whose price is less than or
equal to their bid. No change is given! If you bid 20 and take a prize whose price is 8,
you still pay 20.

All unbought prizes in the display move up one slot. The winner gets to see the next 
prize and decide which empty slot to place it in. Two players will then draw a card
from their money decks: the player who won the auction, and the player who first passed.
